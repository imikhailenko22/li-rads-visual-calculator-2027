What is the LI-RADS Visual Calculator?

This educational prototype explores the proposed CT/MRI LI-RADS v2027 draft approach to categorizing untreated liver observations. Its interactive flow includes imaging modality and contrast agent selection, special observations, major and applicable ancillary imaging features, draft categorization, and editable report text. Its algorithms and draft reporting conventions require independent clinical review and validation.

LI-RADS (Liver Imaging Reporting and Data System) is maintained by the American College of Radiology (ACR). This project is independently developed and is not an official ACR product.

Key features

Exploratory draft classification

Navigate the provisional v2027 untreated-observation algorithm and its size and imaging-feature rules.

Visual imaging references

Review example images and selected pitfalls accompanying major and special imaging features.

Modality-aware feature choices

Explore CT, extracellular-contrast MRI, and hepatobiliary-contrast MRI feature selections.

Draft reporting templates

Generate editable, provisional report wording based on the supplied v2027 draft specifications.

How to use the calculator

  1. Confirm appropriate population and imaging prerequisites by referencing official published LI-RADS guidance; draft criteria are not a substitute.
  2. Select the imaging modality and (for MRI) contrast-agent type, then enter the observation-specific inputs.
  3. Review major and applicable ancillary features and illustrative imaging examples.
  4. Treat the calculated category and report as provisional outputs requiring independent verification.

References and clinical limitations

Refer to the official ACR LI-RADS resources for published criteria and version status. The v2027 content on this site is based on project-supplied draft specifications, not a published or clinically validated ACR CT/MRI release.

Important: Draft and unvalidated. This independent educational prototype is not an official ACR or RSNA application, not a medical device, and must not be used for primary diagnosis or to guide patient management. Verify all decisions against approved published guidance and institutional policy.
